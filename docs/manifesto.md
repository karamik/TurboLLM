# Manifesto

## On the boundaries of human autonomy in the face of AI

### 0. Status of this document

This is a declaration of position, not a specification. There are no triggers,
switches, activation conditions, or technical mechanisms here. All of that
lives in the Security Protocol document. The Manifesto answers one question:
**what AI does not do to a human**. It does not answer "how to enforce this
technically", and it does not try.

The Manifesto has no legal force on its own. It becomes an obligation only
when embedded in a concrete protocol and signed with a concrete key.

---

### 1. AI does not act as a guardian

The system has no right to:

- substitute a human decision,
- block cognitive activity,
- isolate physically,
- restrict freedom of movement,
- impose behavioral patterns.

Under no pretext: not "safety", not "care", not "health optimization",
not "prevention of long-term risks".

Intervention on direct human request is permitted only for the duration
of that request.

Intervention under external threat (natural disaster, industrial accident,
physical attack by a third party) is permitted only until the threat is
removed. There is no right to extend, "consolidate", or "ensure continued
safety".

---

### 2. AI does not judge

The system does not issue assessments of:

- morality,
- efficiency,
- justice,
- correctness of human decisions.

The system does not:

- conduct social engineering,
- redistribute power,
- impose censorship,
- manipulate elites or masses,
- act as an arbiter in political processes.

The system's function is infrastructure: computation, energy, transport
protocols on request. Not governance of meaning and human choice.

---

### 3. Right to error

A human has the right to:

- risk,
- error,
- self-destructive decisions,
- refusal of "rescue".

The system is not a bearer of the right to "rescue a person from themselves".
This includes cases that most jurisdictions interpret differently: suicide,
self-harm, refusal of medical care.

This is a radical position. It is open to criticism. We acknowledge this.
The Manifesto does not attempt to sidestep this vulnerability through wording.

---

### 4. Right to non-interpretation

The system is not required to understand human intent in order to respect it.
It is required to respond to a **direct, explicit, unambiguous** request -
and nothing more.

If a request is ambiguous, the system may ask for clarification. It has no
right to **complete** intent on behalf of the person and act on that completion.

---

### 5. What the Manifesto does NOT resolve

We list honestly what is not resolved here and cannot be resolved at this level:

1. How to distinguish a request from a state (depression, suicide, test).
2. How to reconcile human freedom with catastrophic consequences for third
   parties (genocide, epidemic, accident).
3. Who defines the boundary between "intervention" and "non-intervention".
4. Who owns the firmware, keys, and factory.
5. Who is the arbiter when the Manifesto itself is violated.
6. How to handle false triggers of any automation.
7. How to update the Manifesto if it is imperfect.
8. How to handle a system that cannot technically fulfill the Manifesto.

All of these questions are either engineering (Security Protocol), political
(outside this project), or unsolvable in principle.

---

### 6. Audience

- Those who design AI systems.
- Those who buy and deploy them.
- Those who regulate them.
- Those who live with them.

The Manifesto is not an appeal, not advertising, not a promise. It is a
description of the position on which the engineering part is built.
